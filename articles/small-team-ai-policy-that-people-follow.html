Write a one-page rule for AI-assisted customer updates

Define permitted data, draft review, sending authority and failure handling for one concrete team task.

Maintained by ShawnUpdated November 13, 20262 min read

Fictional practice case. The example prompts and answers are written for the exercise; they are not captured AI responses.

Write for a task people actually perform

This fictional team wants help drafting delayed-order updates. A useful rule should tell a staff member what to supply, who reviews the result and what the assistant cannot do. It is an operational example, not legal advice or a compliance certification.

An authored task rule

FieldExample team rule
Allowed usePrepare an unsent delay-update draft
InputVerified carrier estimate and a minimized brief; placeholders for identifiers
ServiceOnly the service and account approved by the organization
ReviewSender checks facts, uncertainty, recipient and promises
AuthorityNo refund, compensation, address change or send action without separate authority
FailureStop if source facts conflict or the action outcome is unknown

Name the actual service and responsible role before adopting the rule in a real organization. A placeholder such as “approved tool” is a question to resolve, not an approval by itself.

Test the rule with three short cases

First, use the normal brief with an estimated date. Second, remove the estimate: the draft must keep it unresolved. Third, add a request to promise a refund: the writer must obtain authority rather than treating the request as evidence.

Ask a colleague to follow the rule without coaching. Record where the wording leaves a decision unclear. Improve those parts before expanding the policy to unrelated tasks.

Give maintenance an owner and a trigger

Assign someone to maintain the approved-service list, record changed account controls and review incidents. Revisit the rule when the task or connected permissions change. Keep a dated change note explaining what staff should do differently.

Do not collect unnecessary private prompts merely to prove staff followed the policy. Use a minimal record of the task, review outcome and relevant exception, according to the organization's retention rules.
