Task choice

Choose AI tasks by the cost of checking the answer

Apply a four-question decision to ticket routing, email drafting, sales arithmetic, and order changes.

Maintained by ShawnUpdated November 13, 20262 min read

Fictional practice case. The example prompts and answers are written for the exercise; they are not captured AI responses.

Decide at the step level

A workflow can contain both useful drafting tasks and actions that deserve strict controls. Rather than label all email or spreadsheet work suitable for AI, separate the input, proposed output, available check and consequence of error. The examples below are fictional task decisions, not product performance measurements.

TaskIndependent checkSuggested starting point
Rewrite a delay updateCompare with a short verified briefDraft, then review
Route a vague ticketNo clear intent in the messageAsk for clarification
Calculate weekly salesKnown-answer workbook and totalsUse formulas or a deterministic tool
Change delivery addressIdentity and authorization checksUse the approved order process

Ask four concrete questions

First, what data is actually needed? A tone edit needs a brief, not the full customer history. Second, what would count as a correct output? For the sales sample it is a defined date window, status and exact total. Third, how will a person check the result? Fourth, what happens if the check misses an error?

If you cannot name a practical check, reduce the task or keep it with a qualified person. A request to “review your own answer” is useful for finding leads, but does not supply independent evidence.

Run a bounded trial

Use the delivery brief for a writing trial and the sales workbook for a numerical trial. Record setup, working time, review, corrections and whether the output passed. Compare complete results rather than the speed of the first response.

A failed attempt belongs in the log. A second task should test whether the setup is reusable. Avoid extrapolating a small rehearsal into a promise about every employee or every record.

Write the decision as a condition

“Use AI for delay-update drafts when facts are supplied, customer details are minimized, and a person checks every promise” is actionable. “Use AI for customer service” leaves the boundary unclear. Keep the condition with the workflow so later users know when to stop.
