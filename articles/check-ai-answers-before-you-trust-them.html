Verification

Audit an AI-style report claim by claim

Classify four claims against an original workbook, then rewrite the draft without unsupported growth or order counts.

Maintained by ShawnUpdated November 13, 20262 min read

Fictional practice case. The example prompts and answers are written for the exercise; they are not captured AI responses.

Keep the draft and evidence separate

The practice draft says: “Our weekly report proves sales grew 18%. Nine orders produced RM267.60, and all customer identifiers were preserved.” This deliberately faulty text was authored for the exercise. It is not a recorded output from an AI product.

Download the draft and available evidence ↓

Open the worked sales report before deciding which claims can remain. A number matching one cell does not validate the whole paragraph.

Assign a status to each claim

ClaimStatusReason or next check
Sales grew 18%UnestablishedNo prior period or comparable scope supplied
Nine ordersContradictedNine lines represent eight distinct orders
RM267.60Supported within scopeNet Shipped sales for the stated week
All identifiers preservedUnestablished from the summaryInspect source and stored identifier values

Unestablished means the available evidence does not settle the claim. It does not mean the statement has been proved false. Keep that distinction when reviewing an unfamiliar answer.

Write only what the evidence establishes

For 26 October–1 November 2026, the fictional sample contains RM267.60 in net Shipped sales. The report includes 26 units across nine order lines and eight distinct orders.

This authored revision retains the amount and adds its scope. It removes the growth percentage and the preservation claim until their separate checks are completed. If a prior period later becomes available, confirm the same status and date rules before calculating change.

Make the audit reusable

Keep columns for exact claim, source location, applicable date, status and correction. For a source link, open the supporting passage and verify it covers the statement. A citation to a general overview cannot establish a dataset-specific total.

Prioritize claims that affect the decision: totals, eligibility conditions, dates, customer promises and permissions. Asking an assistant to identify possible errors can help organize this work, but the evidence and recalculation must still come from outside the draft.
