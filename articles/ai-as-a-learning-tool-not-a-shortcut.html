Learn SUMIFS by predicting boundary cases first

Use a known-answer sales exercise to test understanding before asking for a formula explanation.

Maintained by ShawnUpdated November 13, 20262 min read

Fictional practice case. The example prompts and answers are written for the exercise; they are not captured AI responses.

Make a prediction before viewing the answer

For the prepared sales sample, predict whether three records belong in the week starting 26 October and ending before 2 November 2026: a Shipped line on 26 October, a Shipped line on 2 November, and a Pending line on 1 November. Write the reasons before asking an assistant.

The first is included, the second is excluded by the end boundary, and the third is excluded by status. This small exercise tests the conditions rather than your ability to copy syntax.

Use the prepared sample ↓

Ask for a hint at the point of confusion

An authored study prompt is: “I understand the status criterion but not why the end date uses less-than. Explain it with one included and one excluded date, without writing the whole workbook.” Compare the explanation with the actual boundary records.

If you want the final formula, ask for a mapping from each criterion to the question it implements. Do not accept “this sums the correct sales” as an explanation of the date logic.

Explain the result in your own words

After working through a case, close the explanation and state why each record was included. Then try the second source file with a new date window. A correct first result may come from memorizing the supplied answer; a changed case tests whether the method transferred.

Keep mistakes in a short learning log: prediction, observed result and corrected rule. Use AI to ask follow-up questions or offer alternative explanations, while the sample and calculation provide the check.
