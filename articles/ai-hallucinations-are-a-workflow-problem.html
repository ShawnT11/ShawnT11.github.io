Catch a missing date condition in a plausible sales answer

Use a RM60.00 discrepancy to show how an answer can be wrong even when its formula runs successfully.

Maintained by ShawnUpdated November 13, 20262 min read

The examples and answer keys on this page are authored teaching material. They are not recorded model outputs or claims about product performance.

A valid calculation can answer the wrong question

The prepared sample contains 13 order lines. Adding every Shipped line produces RM327.60. The requested week, 26 October through before 2 November 2026, produces RM267.60. The RM60.00 difference comes from a RM35.00 line before the window and a RM25.00 line at the excluded end date.

This missing-condition example was authored for teaching. It does not establish that any particular AI model produced the error.

A confident explanation of the first total does not fix its scope. Trace which records enter the calculation and compare them with the stated business question.

Turn the scope into explicit tests

Use an inclusive start, an exclusive end and status exactly Shipped. Check a record on each boundary, a Pending line, a Cancelled line and a legitimate zero-value case. Keep expected outputs before trying a proposed formula.

The sales reconciler shows a decision for each source record. Load the fictional sample and inspect the two date exclusions. The workbook provides a second way to check the result.

Do not reduce every error to invented facts

Errors can come from a mistaken task definition, damaged import, source selection, formula condition or generated wording. Identify the stage before changing a prompt. A better prompt cannot recover an identifier already converted to a different value.

NIST's Generative AI Profile discusses false or inconsistent generated content. This example focuses on a specific numerical check, with no estimate of how often a model fails.
