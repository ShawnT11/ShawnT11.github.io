Prompting

Correct a draft without losing its accurate parts

Mark a wrong order count and unsupported growth claim, then rerun a five-point review.

Maintained by ShawnUpdated November 13, 20262 min read

Fictional practice case. The example prompts and answers are written for the exercise; they are not captured AI responses.

Identify the exact defects

Consider this authored draft: “The business made RM267.60 from nine orders, showing strong growth.” The amount matches the worked sample, but the count should be eight distinct orders across nine lines. No prior-period evidence establishes growth. The sentence also omits the week and Shipped scope.

This is a constructed editing exercise, not an observed response from a particular assistant.

Use the practice draft and evidence ↓

Give a correction with preserved facts

Useful feedback is: “Retain RM267.60. State net Shipped sales for 26 October–1 November 2026. Replace nine orders with nine lines across eight distinct orders. Remove the growth claim because no comparison period is supplied. Use two sentences.”

“Make it more accurate” leaves the required changes implicit. Adding every spreadsheet row would expose more data without improving the writing brief.

Check the whole revised message

For 26 October–1 November 2026, the fictional sample generated RM267.60 in net Shipped sales. The report covers nine order lines across eight distinct orders.

The full review matters because a revision can fix one defect and introduce another. If the message later needs units, add the verified 26 units explicitly.

Stop when the acceptance checks pass

Avoid a long loop of tone edits after the message meets its purpose. Each further revision needs another factual pass. Keep the final version beside the source figures so the sender can verify what is being communicated.

For your own work, save the initial draft, correction instruction, final draft and checklist result. That record shows what changed without pretending every improvement came from a better prompt alone.
