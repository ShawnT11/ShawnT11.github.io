Turn meeting notes into actions without inventing owners

Use a fictional five-statement meeting to distinguish decisions, commitments, proposals and missing assignments.

Maintained by ShawnUpdated November 13, 20262 min read

The examples and answer keys on this page are authored teaching material. They are not recorded model outputs or claims about product performance.

Keep the source statement with the action

The practice notes are from an invented meeting dated 9 November 2026. They contain a decision to use a new export, Dan's commitment to confirm discount meaning by 11 November, an unassigned duplicate review, a possible supplier change and a Friday follow-up.

A clean action table with invented owners is less useful than one that exposes missing assignments. Do not give the duplicate review to Mira merely because she raised it.

Review omissions as well as invented details

Compare the summary with every source statement. The supplier discussion should remain visible as a proposal, even if the action table contains only tasks. Keep decisions and open questions in separate fields or sections so neither disappears.

For a real meeting, confirm ambiguous dates and assignments with participants before treating the register as agreed. An AI summary does not establish consent to a recording or approval to share it.

Use the register at the next meeting

Review unresolved fields, confirm completion evidence for Dan's action, and update the export decision when the reporting week is known. Preserve the original statement if a task changes. This prevents a later summary from rewriting what was actually agreed.

For private meetings, use the approved note-taking process and minimize the material sent to external services. The fictional sample supports practicing the classification without exposing real notes.
