Prompting

Write a report prompt with an acceptance test

Turn checked sales figures into a bounded two-sentence update without inventing growth or confusing lines with orders.

Maintained by ShawnUpdated November 13, 20262 min read

Fictional practice case. The example prompts and answers are written for the exercise; they are not captured AI responses.

Supply the facts before choosing a tone

The fictional weekly report has RM267.60 net Shipped sales, 26 units, nine included lines and eight distinct orders for 26 October–1 November 2026. No prior-period total or explanation for demand is supplied.

An authored prompt is: “Use only these facts. Write two sentences for an operations update. Keep lines and orders distinct. Include the week and Shipped scope. Do not add growth rates, causes or comparisons.” This specification gives the reviewer a concrete result to accept or reject.

Make the checks visible

RequirementPass condition
AmountRM267.60, identified as net sales
ScopeShipped, 26 October–1 November 2026
CountsNine lines across eight orders; 26 units
Unsupported claimsNo growth, demand cause or invented comparison
FormatTwo sentences

A persuasive tone cannot override any factual check. A reply with correct arithmetic but the wrong order count fails.

Compare an acceptable authored answer

For 26 October–1 November 2026, the fictional sample recorded RM267.60 in net Shipped sales. It includes 26 units across nine order lines and eight distinct orders.

This is an authored example, not a captured model output. To test an assistant, save the exact response and score each requirement before revising it. If your real reporting convention differs, rewrite the specification and known answer together.

Keep the output contract with the source

A useful reusable prompt contains fields for purpose, allowed facts, exclusions, output format and acceptance checks. It does not need a fictional expert persona. Use a smaller source when the original contains customer details.

After an edit, rerun all factual checks. A request to shorten the draft can accidentally remove the reporting window or merge counts. Review the final version that will actually be used.
