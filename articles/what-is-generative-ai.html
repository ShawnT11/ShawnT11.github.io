What generative AI adds to an email draft

Use one fictional delivery brief to separate supplied facts, generated wording, and promises requiring approval.

Maintained by ShawnUpdated November 13, 20262 min read

The examples and answer keys on this page are authored teaching material. They are not recorded model outputs or claims about product performance.

The input is a brief, not a private thread

Generative AI produces content such as text or images from an input. In an email workflow, its useful contribution can be wording and organization. Whether the parcel will arrive is a separate question requiring evidence from the carrier.

Download the fictional delivery brief ↓

The brief supplies an estimated delivery date of 20 November 2026. It does not supply the customer's name, an order number, a guaranteed arrival, or permission to promise compensation.

Create a draft with explicit boundaries

An authored prompt for this exercise is: “Use only the attached fictional brief. Write a calm update of at most 90 words. Keep name and order-reference placeholders. Describe 20 November as the carrier's estimate. Do not promise a refund, compensation or guaranteed delivery.” This is a teaching prompt, not a record of a model test.

Hello [name], I’m writing about order [reference]. The carrier currently estimates delivery on 20 November 2026. This is an estimate, and the date may change. I’m sorry for the delay. Please contact us if you need a further status update.

The paragraph above is an authored acceptable answer. It shows one possible outcome rather than predicting what any assistant will generate.

Separate wording from new facts

Draft elementWhere it comes fromReview decision
Delivery estimateSupplied briefRetain the uncertainty
Greeting and apologyAuthored wordingCheck tone
Guaranteed arrivalNo evidenceRemove
Refund offerNo authority suppliedRemove or obtain approval
Customer nameNot suppliedKeep a placeholder

A shorter draft is not necessarily better if it drops the word estimate. A warmer draft is not acceptable if it invents a compensation offer. Judge the final message against the brief, then review it before sending.

Reuse the specification, not the customer record

Keep the purpose, factual fields and approval limits as a reusable brief. Replace facts for each case. Do not copy yesterday's estimate into today's message. For higher-stakes communication, the person responsible must verify the actual facts and authorization.
