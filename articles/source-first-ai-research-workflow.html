Build a source ledger before asking AI to summarize a policy

Compare current, archived, and irrelevant sources using a fictional office-access question.

Maintained by ShawnUpdated November 13, 20262 min read

Fictional practice case. The example prompts and answers are written for the exercise; they are not captured AI responses.

Write a question with a date and scope

Use this precise question: “For an ordinary visitor on 13 November 2026, what entry time and conditions does the supplied policy establish?” It specifies a person category and a decision date. That prevents a cafeteria schedule or an archived rule from becoming the answer by accident.

For real research, replace these IDs with direct source links, section names, publication or effective dates and access dates. Keep a short supporting excerpt within permitted quotation limits.

Ask for a bounded synthesis

An authored prompt is: “Answer using only the ledger and source pack. Cite the source ID for each rule. Separate ordinary visitors from maintenance visits. State missing information rather than supplying it from memory.” Review the answer against the pack after generation.

The accepted ordinary-visitor answer is 10:00–16:00 with a named host. The pack does not answer weekend access or explain how host approval is obtained. Those are follow-up questions for the policy owner.

Maintain the evidence when sources change

If a new policy supersedes P2, update its status and revisit answers that rely on it. Keep the previous conclusion with its original date so a later reader can understand why it changed. A source ledger is useful because it exposes these dependencies.

This teaching case contains no real employment, legal or access advice. For an actual organization, use its authoritative source and approved research process.
